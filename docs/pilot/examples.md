# What the pilot can distinguish

These are first-pass examples from the frozen random sample, not adjudicated ratings or prevalence estimates. They illustrate why the new unit of measurement is the challenged inference rather than the cited article.

## A candidate for consequential reliance

A 2024 *Nature* paper, [“Myelin plasticity in the ventral tegmental area is required for opioid reward”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07525-7), cites [Tsai et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1168878) while motivating its comparison of phasic and tonic dopamine-neuron stimulation. In the first Results section, it describes tonic stimulation as not producing reward or aversion and attaches the original paper as reference 30. The study then uses those stimulation conditions to investigate oligodendroglial responses.

That is a concrete place to investigate whether a disputed inference influences a subsequent design. The original classification flags the phasic-versus-tonic behavioral comparison. The original article's Figure 4 reports a significant conditioning change in the phasic cohort and a nonsignificant result in the tonic cohort; separate tests do not establish the difference between changes, and a nonsignificant result does not establish no effect.

This example still needs independent review of the original supplementary analyses, subsequent evidence, and the complete citing paper. It does **not** establish that the later study's findings are invalid, that its authors knew of the criticism, or that the underlying claim is false. The appropriate question is whether the particular statement and use are supported by the evidence the later paper invokes.

Sample pair: `nw_97__W4399367191`. Relevant locations: citing paper's first Results section and optogenetic-stimulation Methods; original paper's Figure 4A–C and accompanying text.

## A consequential use that need not propagate the flagged error

A 2019 computational study, [“Reservoir computing model of prefrontal cortex creates novel combinations of previous navigation sequences from hippocampal place-cell replay with spatial reward propagation”](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006624), uses [Singer and Frank (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.11.016) to motivate reward-modulated hippocampal replay in a learning model.

The original reviewers' flagged comparison concerns how a novel-versus-familiar sequence difference changes across training days. That is narrower than the proposition that reward modulates replay. A later paper can therefore use the original research substantively—even to motivate a model—without using the particular inference flagged in the audit. Coding it as error propagation merely because the reference is to a flagged paper would overstate what the evidence shows.

Sample pair: `nw_105__W2957923780`. Relevant location: citing paper's introduction, paragraph on replay and reinforcement learning, reference 18; original paper's Figure 3b,c and first-versus-third-day comparison.

These cases make the proposed extension more informative than another citation-count comparison. It can distinguish the continued scientific use of a research contribution from continued reliance on its particular unsupported inference, and identify when the latter reaches a design or modeling decision.
