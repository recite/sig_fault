# A study across methodological critiques

The central question is whether public methodological criticism changes the subsequent use of the criticized research. The first scalable outcome is citation evolution. Citation-context coding can then establish whether later papers use the challenged finding, qualify it, or cite a different contribution.

The project should repeat the same citation design across a set of critiques, with each critique supplying an assessed-paper population and interpretable comparison groups. Choose critiques before looking at their citation effects. Keep study-specific definitions and timelines visible, and report differences across audits before attempting a pooled average.

## The third cohort needs its own comparison definition

The Hainmueller–Mummolo–Xu appendix identifies the papers and separately reports three diagnostics. A preliminary reading of an “any adverse interaction” rule produces 20 adverse papers and only two entirely zero-score papers, Huddy/Mason/Aarøe and Tavits. Some papers have mixed findings and some tests are unavailable. The broad binary contrast would therefore have a weak comparison group. Separate diagnostic contrasts are preferable; unavailable tests must remain unknown.

The [2019 corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2019.17) completes a bibliographic reference and does not revise diagnostic classifications. The [authors' 2025 response](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05717v1) clarifies estimands and assumptions and acknowledges potential binning bias, without supplying a revised 22-paper classification. Before calling a historical flag an established error, check whether the diagnostic addresses the original claim and how subsequent methodological discussion affects it.

The [SSRN record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2739221) dates first posting to February 29, 2016 and the accessible revision to April 29, 2018. The [journal article](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2018.46) appeared online December 18, 2018. Its 2019 issue date is not the first-publication date. Establish when each assessed paper entered the critique before defining a sharper publicity event. The [author-hosted paper and supplements](https://jmummolo.scholar.princeton.edu/document/158) supply the roster; the [replication archive](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/Q1V0OG) still needs file-level inspection.

## Common design and aggregation

For each audit, identify the full assessed population, freeze the diagnostic definitions, resolve article identities, document earliest circulation and formal publication, and collect complete citation histories through a common full-year cutoff. Count journal articles and reviews consistently; separately report preprints and books with explicit version and publication-unit rules. Lead with fixed-cohort means and medians, then relative and absolute changes and their uncertainty. Use article and year effects within each audit; inspect citation aging, pre-period patterns, and sensitivity to the warning date.

Before pooling, refresh the full Nieuwenhuis cohort using the same OpenAlex extraction and check it against the historical Web of Science exports. The current two analyses use different citation sources and follow-up lengths. Show a common one-full-year follow-up where feasible, alongside each audit's longer available history; do not attribute differences in index coverage or observation windows to scientific responses.

A combined study should first show each audit's result. Equal-audit weighting asks about the typical included critique; equal-paper weighting asks about the typical assessed paper in the assembled sample. Neither automatically represents all scientific errors. Track papers shared between audits and avoid counting the same citation history as independent evidence. Heterogeneity by diagnostic seriousness, paper-specific identification, prominence of the warning, and correction versus general criticism is substantively informative, but few audits cannot support an elaborate moderator model.

The inclusion criterion is recoverable data and a defensible comparison, not whether a case confirms continued citation. The Lal results already show why: different diagnostics within one audit yield different citation patterns.
