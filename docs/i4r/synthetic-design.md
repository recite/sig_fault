# Synthetic-control sensitivity for the matched I4R cases

Recorded October 6, 2026 before fitting synthetic weights. The four supported cases, their annual outcomes, conventional matched estimates and proportional summaries are already known. This is a retrospective design check, not a preregistration. Its purpose is to determine whether pre-disclosure citation paths support a more credible counterfactual for each affected paper.

Use the same four cases supported by the secondary annual-total data. Preserve the first substantiated public-disclosure year, the exclusion of the disclosure year and the +1 full-calendar-year outcome. The outcome remains OpenAlex all-type annual citations; this does not complete the separate article/review analysis.

For each target, use every available full pre-disclosure year from the later of 2017 and the year after the original paper's publication. Donors must satisfy the existing journal, publication-year proximity, article-type, verified-identity, assessment, retraction and research-publication restrictions. Remove the original matcher's citation caliper and text-distance ranking: neither selects synthetic donors. Require donors to be published before the first fitting year. Require complete annual counts for every otherwise eligible donor; do not select donors based on post-disclosure outcomes. The complete-pool gate from the existing annual analysis remains in force.

Require at least three full pre-disclosure years to attempt a fit. Fit nonnegative donor weights summing to one by minimizing mean squared pre-period citation discrepancies plus a fixed ridge penalty on squared weights. Scale both target and donor counts by the target's pre-period root mean square (or one if all zero); use penalty `1e-6`. The penalty selects a stable solution when many donor combinations fit a short history, and is not tuned to the post-disclosure result. Verify optimizer convergence, simplex constraints, objective improvement over uniform weights and a convex first-order optimality bound. Report the full weights, their concentration, pre-period RMS error and post-disclosure gap.

First withhold the last pre-disclosure year, fit on the earlier years, and report the prediction error in that held-out year. Donor eligibility may use identity and publication timing but not that year's citations. Refit all pre-disclosure years for the post-disclosure comparison. A good fitted trajectory with a poor held-out prediction is evidence against relying on that synthetic control. Do not discard cases based on this result or choose specifications by their post-disclosure estimates.

Show the post-disclosure count difference and, separately, the change in the target-minus-synthetic gap from the final pre-period year. Report a fixed larger ridge penalty (`0.01`) and refitting after dropping the largest-weight donor as sensitivities. Do not pool these selected case gaps into a headline causal estimate or attach permutation p-values without a defensible exchangeability argument. In-space placebo comparisons can be diagnostic, but a short pre-history and nonrandom error disclosure do not provide a randomization distribution.

Identification would require the weighted donor path to approximate the target's unobserved citation trajectory without publicity, with no coincident target-specific shocks, anticipation or donor exposure to the same warning. These assumptions are not established by optimizing pre-fit. Original publication age, changing scientific relevance and different citation dynamics can defeat the comparison. The inventor-clusters case currently has only two full pre-years and must remain explicitly unsupported by this check.

Methodological reference: Alberto Abadie, [Using Synthetic Controls: Feasibility, Data Requirements, and Methodological Aspects](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20191450). The convex quadratic program uses [OSQP](https://osqp.org/docs/interfaces/python.html), with explicit residual variables and a first-order optimality gate. The solver must resolve the small ridge penalty as well as citation fit; a successful termination code alone is insufficient.
