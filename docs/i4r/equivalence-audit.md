# Equivalence-testing audit: article roster and remaining evidence

[Fitzgerald's May 2024 audit](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/296190/1/I4R-DP125.pdf) reanalyzes statistical evidence used to support claims of negligible or nonexistent relationships. The report describes 876 models supporting 135 claims in 81 papers. These are assessed papers; the roster does not establish which have material errors.

The 81 records in `assessment_inventory.json` are identified by `dp_125` in `source_ids`: 80 new records have unit IDs beginning `i4r_dp125_`, and one links the existing `i4r_dp158_assessment`. Each records the original article DOI, Jack Fitzgerald as reviewer, the source document and the page listing that article. All 81 identities match publisher metadata or previously verified registry identities. Eighteen papers already appeared under other assessments; 63 are new original articles. Assessments by other teams remain separate. Fitzgerald also reviewed the climate-innovation paper in DP158; the two reports share one reviewer/article unit. The previous DP158 robustness-dispute adjudication remains recorded, while the unresolved DP125 findings prevent treating the combined unit as fully reviewed.

## Source reconciliation

Appendix B (PDF pages 51–66) explicitly identifies the final sample. We retain journal articles and exclude linked datasets and code packages. We also exclude Casas-Arce and Saiz (2015), DOI `10.1086/680686`: the appendix introduction explicitly says that it supplies data for an assessed paper but is not itself in this sample. The resulting 81 distinct DOIs agree with the reported final-sample size.

The earlier screening universe is not fully recoverable from this version's bibliography. The methods describe attempted reproductions of 2,346 models supporting 279 claims in 158 articles (PDF pages 20–22 and 49–50). Appendix C (PDF pages 67–75) says it lists papers outside the final sample, but its 81 article DOIs exactly repeat the final sample. We do not count those references as additional assessments or infer identities for the remaining 77 articles. This inconsistency leaves DP125's full scope unresolved.

## What can enter the analysis

Roster inclusion establishes that a paper was assessed, not that an equivalence test failed. Even a failed equivalence test would not demonstrate that the true effect is nonzero or that the paper contains a material error. We need article-specific claims, test results, the chosen negligible-effect thresholds and a defensible public-disclosure date before assigning an exposure classification. No new error event or treatment-effect estimate is created from aggregate failure rates.

Fifteen of the newly identified articles have cached control-candidate records. Matching now excludes those identities using the current assessment registry, while retaining their original metadata and citation histories. This repeats the existing conservative control-acquisition rule at analysis time. It prevents registry updates from leaving newly recognized assessed papers eligible as controls. The source roster and registry change were selected from audit evidence, without using citation outcomes to decide inclusion.

The candidate screen affects 14 distinct papers across the supported event windows; the fifteenth does not reach those windows' candidate comparisons. None was a previously selected nearest control, but removing them changes the pool used to scale citation and text distances. One inventor-clusters match consequently changes, reducing that case's contrast from +37.33 to +29.00 citations and the four-case average from +7.67 to +5.58. The three other case contrasts are unchanged. The synthetic-control fits are unchanged; the unsupported inventor-clusters donor pool falls from 209 to 198. These revisions leave the interpretation unchanged: citation use persists, while the small, selected comparisons do not identify a stable publicity effect.
