# Another pseudoreplication inventory checked

We also inspected the deposited materials for Whitehouse et al.,
[“Pseudoreplication in Primate Communication Research: 10 Years On”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10764-023-00399-y),
available at [OSF](https://osf.io/scdfx/). This extends the earlier Waller et al.
audit of primate research. It is a candidate source, not a landed set of identifiable
papers or an additional treatment-effect sample.

The recovered `df_full.csv` has 592 rows: 309 old and 283 new records;
`df_newonly.csv` repeats the 283 new records. The deposited R Markdown script
documents the analysis. Journal and author names are replaced by labels such as
`journal_002` and `author_002`. Neither file supplies article titles, DOIs or PMIDs.
The public records therefore cannot currently be linked reliably to article-level
citation histories. Their analyzed subsets also should not be described as complete
inventories of every paper screened in the two audits.

Resolving this requires an article-identity key from the authors or another public
source. No speculative title matching or reverse identification has been attempted.
The downloaded files, OSF node/file metadata, retrieval sidecars and checksums are
preserved in `private-data/inventories/primate-review/`. No author contact has been
made. The identified 200-paper Lazic cohort is the immediately usable expansion.
